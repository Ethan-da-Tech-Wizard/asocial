# First 7 Scripts

Last updated: September 14, 2026

These are starter scripts for the first week. They are written for 45 to 75 second short-form videos. Use your normal speaking voice. If a line feels too polished, simplify it before recording.

## Script 1: Leaving Work You Hate

Pillar: Work and survival.
Format: Nobody tells you this about...
Viewer payoff: Seen.
Source level: Level 1, lived experience and opinion.
Claim risk: Low.

Hook:

Nobody tells you this about leaving work you hate.

Script:

Nobody tells you this about leaving work you hate: wanting out is only the first part.

The harder part is replacing what that job gave you, even if the job was draining you. It gave you a paycheck, a schedule, a routine, and this weird sense of safety, even when you did not feel safe at all.

So when people say, "Just quit," they miss the real problem. Most people are not staying because they love it. They are staying because rent is real, bills are real, fear is real, and starting over can feel expensive before it ever pays off.

That is why the escape plan needs a system, not just frustration.

For me, that means building something low-cost, repeatable, and trackable instead of just hoping motivation carries it.

If you are trying to get out too, you are not weak for needing a plan. You are being realistic.

Caption:

Leaving a draining job takes more than motivation. It takes a system.

## Script 2: Healthcare Work People Do Not Explain

Pillar: Systems explained.
Format: Here is the part they do not explain...
Viewer payoff: Seen.
Source level: Level 1, lived experience and opinion.
Claim risk: Medium. Avoid naming employers, patients, or specific private incidents.

Hook:

Here is the part of healthcare work people do not explain.

Script:

Here is the part of healthcare work people do not explain: being praised does not always mean being supported.

A lot of healthcare workers hear words like essential, caring, strong, and selfless. But behind that, many people are dealing with heavy workloads, emotional stress, difficult schedules, and the pressure of knowing real people depend on them.

That kind of work can make you feel proud and exhausted at the same time.

And when someone burns out, people act like it came out of nowhere. But a lot of the time, it was building for months or years. The body keeps score. The mind does too.

This is not about blaming patients or saying the work does not matter. It matters a lot. That is exactly why the people doing it need to be treated like humans, not unlimited resources.

If you have worked in care work, you probably know exactly what I mean.

Caption:

Healthcare workers can care deeply and still be completely worn down.

## Script 3: A Job Can Pay You And Still Keep You Stuck

Pillar: Work and survival.
Format: Nobody tells you this about...
Viewer payoff: Informed.
Source level: Level 1, lived experience and opinion.
Claim risk: Low.

Hook:

A job can pay you and still keep you stuck.

Script:

A job can pay you and still keep you stuck.

That sounds backwards until you look at what work actually takes from you besides hours. It can take your energy, your sleep, your schedule, your patience, and the little bit of focus you would need to build something better.

So yes, the paycheck matters. But the paycheck is not the whole picture.

If the job pays just enough to keep you coming back, but not enough to give you margin, you can end up surviving without actually moving.

That is the trap a lot of people are trying to explain when they say they feel stuck. They are not saying they do not want to work. They are saying work is taking so much from them that they cannot create another path.

That is why time and energy are part of income too.

Caption:

Income matters, but so does whether the job leaves you any room to move.

## Script 4: Tired From Surviving

Pillar: Work and survival.
Format: Nobody tells you this about...
Viewer payoff: Seen.
Source level: Level 1, lived experience and opinion.
Claim risk: Low.

Hook:

Nobody tells you how tired you can be from surviving.

Script:

Nobody tells you how tired you can be from surviving.

Not from doing one huge thing. From constantly calculating. Can I pay this bill? Can I wait on that one? Do I have gas? Can I handle one more shift? What happens if something breaks?

That kind of pressure does not always look dramatic from the outside. Sometimes it just looks like procrastination, irritability, or being too tired to start anything new.

But survival mode uses energy.

That is why telling people to just hustle harder misses the point. A person who is already stretched thin does not only need motivation. They need a system that lowers friction.

Small repeatable steps matter because they do not require you to become a different person overnight.

If you are tired from trying to keep everything together, that does not mean you are lazy. It means the load has been heavy.

Caption:

Survival mode takes energy. Build systems that respect that.

## Script 5: Social Media Money

Pillar: Systems explained.
Format: Here is the part they do not explain...
Viewer payoff: Informed.
Source level: Level 3, platform sources recommended.
Claim risk: High. Confirm current platform rules before posting.

Hook:

Here is the part they do not explain about social media money.

Script:

Here is the part they do not explain about social media money: views and money are not the same thing.

A video can get views and still make nothing if the account is not eligible, the views are not qualified, the video is too short for a certain program, or the platform does not pay that format the way people think it does.

That is why chasing views alone can be misleading.

The real game is attention plus trust plus eligibility plus a way to turn that audience into income over time.

For short-form content, the first job is usually discovery. You find out what people care about, what they watch, what they share, and what makes them follow.

Then the stronger topics can become longer videos, better explanations, sponsor opportunities, affiliate ideas, or simple products later.

So if you are trying to make money online, do not only ask, "How many views did it get?"

Ask, "What did this prove?"

Caption:

Views are signal first. Money comes from eligibility, trust, and a real income path.

## Script 6: Being Broke Gets More Expensive

Pillar: Money awareness.
Format: The quiet way this costs people...
Viewer payoff: Warned.
Source level: Level 2, reputable sources recommended if adding statistics.
Claim risk: Medium.

Hook:

The quiet way being broke gets more expensive.

Script:

The quiet way being broke gets more expensive is that problems start charging late fees.

If you have money, you can buy the bigger pack, fix the car early, pay the bill on time, avoid the overdraft, and choose the cheaper option before it becomes urgent.

But when money is tight, everything turns into timing.

You pay more because you had to wait. You pay more because you could not buy in bulk. You pay more because transportation broke at the worst time. You pay more because the bill hit before the paycheck did.

That is why people call it expensive to be poor.

It is not about saying every choice is out of your control. It is about noticing that pressure changes the options available to you.

And once you see that pattern, you can stop blaming yourself for every single cost and start looking for the few places where you can create a little more room.

Caption:

Being broke can make ordinary problems more expensive.

## Script 7: First 30 Days Recap

Pillar: Systems explained.
Format: Here is the part they do not explain...
Viewer payoff: Next step.
Source level: Level 1, personal experiment.
Claim risk: Low.

Hook:

Here is what I learned after 30 days of trying to build income online.

Script:

Here is what I learned after 30 days of trying to build income online.

The biggest lesson is that posting is not the whole job. Tracking is the job too.

Because one video getting views does not automatically mean you have a business. You have to know what people responded to. Was it the topic? The hook? The format? The emotion? The platform?

That is what I am trying to figure out.

The goal is not to pretend this is easy. The goal is to build a system that can tell me what is worth repeating and what needs to change.

So I am looking at comments, shares, saves, followers, watch time, and which topics people actually care enough to respond to.

If you are trying to build something from scratch too, do not just create. Pay attention to the evidence.

Caption:

The first goal is not perfection. It is evidence.
