# 14 Two-Sided Conflict Media Experiment

Last updated: September 14, 2026

## Decision

The project can test two Facebook Pages only if both are transparent, original, sourced, and moderated.

Do not create fake grassroots personas. Do not pretend the Pages are run by different people if they are not. Do not manufacture outrage, impersonate communities, or hide that this is a media-framing experiment.

Recommended setup:

> Two sister Pages studying how audiences respond to Israel/Palestine-related media framing, with clear disclosure that both are part of the same public media experiment.

## Why This Needs Different Rules

Israel/Palestine content is high-risk. It can easily slide into:

- Hate speech.
- Dehumanization.
- Violence or incitement.
- Misinformation.
- Graphic or exploitative tragedy content.
- Harassment.
- Dangerous organization praise or support.
- Political or social issue ad restrictions.

This means the experiment cannot be run like a normal engagement page. The goal should be discussion, media literacy, and audience research, not rage farming.

## Page Structure

Use two clearly labeled Pages:

1. Israel Lens Media Watch.
2. Palestine Lens Media Watch.

Alternative names:

- Israel Narrative Watch.
- Palestine Narrative Watch.
- Israel Media Lens.
- Palestine Media Lens.

Each Page bio should include the same disclosure:

> Part of a two-sided media experiment studying how people respond to Israel/Palestine coverage, framing, and public narratives. We use sources, avoid hate, and invite serious discussion.

Each Page should link to the other Page in the About section if possible.

## What Each Page Covers

Israel Lens Media Watch:

- Israeli civilian safety.
- Antisemitism and Jewish community concerns.
- Israeli government and military decisions.
- Hostage-related media coverage.
- How Western media frames Israeli perspectives.
- How social platforms handle anti-Israel and antisemitic content.

Palestine Lens Media Watch:

- Palestinian civilian safety.
- Islamophobia, anti-Arab racism, and Palestinian community concerns.
- Israeli occupation, displacement, aid, blockade, and humanitarian coverage.
- Gaza and West Bank reporting.
- How Western media frames Palestinian perspectives.
- How social platforms handle pro-Palestinian and anti-Palestinian content.

## Non-Negotiable Editorial Rules

Both Pages must follow the same rules:

- Humanize civilians on both sides.
- Criticize governments, militaries, parties, policies, and media framing without attacking people based on ethnicity, nationality, or religion.
- Do not celebrate death, injury, displacement, humiliation, or fear.
- Do not praise, endorse, or recruit for violent organizations.
- Do not post graphic content for shock value.
- Do not claim breaking news unless verified by reliable sources.
- Do not use AI-generated realistic scenes of war, victims, protests, or violence.
- Do not dox, harass, or target private individuals.
- Correct mistakes publicly.

## Allowed Angles

Good angles:

- "Notice how this headline frames agency."
- "Here is what this source says, and what it leaves out."
- "Two outlets covered the same event differently."
- "This term changes how people understand the story."
- "This claim needs a source before people repeat it."
- "This comment section shows what people are reacting to."

Risky angles:

- "They all think..."
- "These people always..."
- "This proves they are evil..."
- "Everyone on that side supports..."
- "The media is hiding everything..."
- "Share before they delete this..."

## Source Rules

Every post should have a source level:

- Level 1: commentary about framing, language, or audience reaction.
- Level 2: reputable news report or expert analysis.
- Level 3: primary source, official statement, court filing, humanitarian organization report, platform policy, or direct transcript.

Required Level 3 topics:

- Death tolls.
- Injuries.
- Hostage claims.
- Aid claims.
- Military claims.
- Legal claims.
- Genocide, war crime, terrorism, or atrocity claims.
- Platform policy claims.
- Breaking-news corrections.

If a claim cannot be sourced, frame it as a question or do not post it.

## Moderation Rules

Delete or hide comments that:

- Dehumanize Israelis, Palestinians, Jews, Muslims, Arabs, or any protected group.
- Celebrate civilian harm.
- Call for violence.
- Use slurs.
- Dox private people.
- Spam propaganda slogans without discussion.
- Harass other commenters.

Reply to comments that:

- Ask for sources.
- Point out missing context.
- Share a personal perspective respectfully.
- Challenge framing without hate.

Pin a comment on heated posts:

> Discuss the framing, source, or claim. Do not attack civilians, religions, ethnic groups, or other commenters.

## Experiment Design

The experiment is not "which side wins."

The experiment is:

> How do different audiences respond to different frames, sources, emotions, and media formats around the same conflict?

Track:

- Page.
- Topic.
- Source level.
- Frame type.
- Emotional tone.
- Comment quality.
- Share rate.
- Follow rate.
- Hide/delete count.
- Whether the discussion stayed civil.

Frame types:

- Civilian harm.
- Media framing.
- Policy decision.
- Historical context.
- Platform moderation.
- Humanitarian impact.
- Community safety.
- Misleading claim check.

Emotional tones:

- Grief.
- Warning.
- Context.
- Accountability.
- Humanization.
- Source check.

## 90-Day Two-Page Plan

### Days 1-14: Setup And Calibration

Goal:

> Test whether two Pages can be run safely without turning into chaos.

Actions:

- Create both Pages with matching disclosure.
- Publish 3 posts per Page per week.
- Use only sourced or framing-analysis posts.
- Avoid breaking news unless verified.
- Moderate daily.
- Track comment quality, not just views.

Exit rule:

If either Page attracts mostly hate, threats, or dehumanizing comments, pause the experiment and tighten the format.

### Days 15-45: Paired Topic Testing

Goal:

> Compare how different audiences respond to similar topics.

Actions:

- Pick one shared topic each week.
- Publish one Israel-lens post and one Palestine-lens post.
- Use similar structure and length.
- Track differences in comments, shares, and follows.
- Publish one neutral recap post in private notes or a third document, not as rage content.

Example paired topic:

- How headlines describe civilian deaths.
- How outlets use passive voice.
- How social platforms moderate conflict speech.
- How aid access is framed.
- How diaspora communities talk about safety.

### Days 46-90: Keep, Merge, Or Stop

Goal:

> Decide whether the two-Page structure produces useful public discussion or just splits attention.

Actions:

- Continue only the formats that produce civil discussion.
- Stop formats that generate hate without insight.
- Consider merging into one "Conflict Media Watch" Page if two Pages become too hard to moderate.
- Do not monetize through inflammatory content.

By Day 90, decide:

- Keep two Pages.
- Merge into one Page.
- Stop the conflict experiment.
- Return to the original work/money/systems Facebook plan.

## Hard Stop Rules

Pause immediately if:

- The Pages become mostly dehumanizing comment sections.
- You feel pulled into rage posting.
- You cannot moderate comments daily.
- You cannot verify claims before posting.
- Either Page receives repeated policy warnings.
- The content starts rewarding civilian suffering as engagement.

## Verdict

Two Pages can be tested, but only as a disclosed media-framing experiment.

The safer and stronger structure is not "pro-Israel vs pro-Palestine propaganda." It is:

> Israel Lens Media Watch and Palestine Lens Media Watch, both run by the same standards, both focused on media framing, source checking, civilian humanization, and public reaction.

If the goal is to get people talking, the Pages must give them something better to talk about than hatred.
