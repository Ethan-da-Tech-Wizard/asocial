# 05 Risks And Ethics

Last updated: September 14, 2026

## Major Risks

### 1. Platform Dependence

If the project relies on one platform, a rule change, account issue, or algorithm shift could damage the income plan.

Mitigation:

- Post across several platforms.
- Build YouTube long-form over time.
- Eventually collect emails or community members outside platforms.

### 2. Low-Value Reposting

Platforms are increasingly strict about copied, minimally modified, or low-originality content.

Mitigation:

- Use original voice, analysis, framing, or lived experience.
- Avoid simply reuploading clips.
- Keep source links and notes.
- Add meaningful explanation when discussing third-party material.

### 3. Misinformation

Public-awareness content can easily become misleading if speed matters more than accuracy.

Mitigation:

- Verify claims before posting.
- Distinguish fact from opinion.
- Use cautious language when uncertain.
- Correct mistakes publicly when needed.

### 4. Burnout

The creator is already trying to escape draining work. Content creation should not become another unsustainable grind.

Mitigation:

- Use repeatable formats.
- Batch ideas and scripts.
- Keep the first posting goal realistic.
- Review performance weekly, not obsessively every hour.

### 5. Monetization Delay

Platform payouts may take months or longer. Early views may not produce income.

Mitigation:

- Treat first 30 to 90 days as validation.
- Track eligibility.
- Explore affiliate offers, sponsorships, services, and digital products after audience signals appear.

### 6. Rage-Bait Trap

Anger can drive views, but a brand built only on outrage can become stressful, less trustworthy, and less sponsor-friendly.

Mitigation:

- Use urgency without manipulation.
- Explain, do not only inflame.
- Make the viewer feel smarter, not just angrier.

## Ethical Standards

- Do not knowingly post false claims.
- Do not impersonate sources or creators.
- Do not steal videos.
- Do not fake screenshots.
- Do not hide sponsorships or affiliate relationships.
- Do not exploit tragedy for empty views.
- Do not give medical, legal, or financial advice beyond your knowledge.
- When discussing high-stakes topics, point people to primary sources.

## Brand Trust Rules

The account should earn the right to be believed.

Trust-building habits:

- Say "I do not know" when appropriate.
- Show sources when making factual claims.
- Explain uncertainty.
- Admit corrections.
- Avoid pretending to be an expert in everything.
- Keep the audience's life in mind, not just the algorithm.
