# 02 Platform Research

Last updated: September 14, 2026

This research focuses on social platforms that can turn attention into income. Exact creator earnings vary heavily by niche, country, audience quality, advertiser demand, watch time, and platform rules. Most platforms do not pay directly for likes or reactions. Reactions usually help by increasing distribution, qualified views, retention, or community trust.

## Platform Ranking For This Project

### 1. YouTube Long-Form

Best use: Long-term income engine.

YouTube is usually the strongest platform for direct ad revenue because long-form videos can earn through watch-page ads and YouTube Premium. YouTube states that partners can earn from ads, memberships, Shopping, Super Chat, Super Stickers, Super Thanks, and Premium. Under the Watch Page Monetization Module, creators can receive 55% of net revenue from ads shown on eligible public videos.

Why it matters:

- Better long-term monetization than most short-form platforms.
- Search and recommendation can keep older videos alive.
- Longer videos allow deeper explanation and trust building.
- Can later support memberships, products, and sponsorships.

Challenge:

- Harder to produce than short-form.
- Requires stronger scripting, titles, thumbnails, and retention.
- Monetization eligibility takes time.

Best role in the strategy:

Use short-form posts to test topics. Turn winning topics into longer YouTube videos.

Source:

- YouTube Partner Earnings Overview: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72902

### 2. Facebook

Best use: Strong monetization upside for original public-awareness content.

Facebook Content Monetization pays across multiple eligible formats, including Reels, longer videos, Stories, photos, and text posts. Meta has stated that future earnings emphasis includes original content, deeper engagement, longer watch time, and qualified views. Meta also reported that Facebook paid creators nearly $3 billion in 2025.

Why it matters:

- Monetization is not limited to one format.
- Facebook audiences can respond well to practical, social, local, and "you need to know this" content.
- Shares can travel far when content feels useful or urgent.

Challenge:

- Facebook Content Monetization can be eligibility or invite based.
- Originality rules matter. Low-effort reactions, reposts, and lightly edited third-party content can be deprioritized or demonetized.

Best role in the strategy:

Post original Reels and text/photo explainers. Use Facebook as a serious monetization candidate, not just a reposting dump.

Sources:

- Meta Creator Fast Track / Facebook Content Monetization: https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/creator-fast-track-grow-your-audience-earn-money-on-facebook/
- Meta Original Creator Guidelines: https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/rewarding-original-creators-on-facebook/

### 3. TikTok

Best use: Fast discovery and topic testing.

TikTok's Creator Rewards Program replaced the old Creator Fund. TikTok says qualifying videos must be original, high quality, and at least one minute long. TikTok rewards are based on qualified views and RPM, with factors such as watch time, completion rate, search value, viewer location, engagement, and ad value.

Common eligibility requirements include being at least 18, having at least 10,000 followers, at least 100,000 video views in the last 30 days, and posting eligible original videos of at least one minute.

Why it matters:

- Strong discovery for new creators.
- Good for testing hooks, topics, and formats quickly.
- Search value matters, which fits explanatory content.

Challenge:

- Payouts can vary a lot.
- Videos under one minute generally do not fit Creator Rewards monetization.
- Viral attention may not equal stable income.

Best role in the strategy:

Use TikTok to test public-awareness topics and develop repeatable short-form formats. Favor videos over one minute once eligible for rewards.

### 4. YouTube Shorts

Best use: Discovery and funnel into long-form YouTube.

YouTube Shorts monetization uses a pooled revenue model. Shorts creators can receive 45% of the revenue allocated to them from the Shorts Creator Pool. YouTube calculates payments based on eligible engaged views, country, music usage, and the creator's share of views.

Important future change:

Starting February 1, 2027, YouTube says Shorts Creator Pool earnings will require 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days. New YPP ad and Premium entry thresholds are also scheduled to change to 1,000 subscribers plus either 8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days or 20 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days.

Why it matters:

- Useful for discovery.
- Can feed viewers into long-form videos.
- Works well for repurposed short-form content.

Challenge:

- Per-view earnings are usually lower than long-form.
- Monetization requirements are becoming harder for Shorts.

Best role in the strategy:

Post Shorts consistently, but do not make Shorts the only income plan.

Sources:

- YouTube Shorts Monetization: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220
- YouTube Partner Program Changes: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009

### 5. Instagram

Best use: Trust, identity, community, brand deals, and fan monetization.

Instagram is useful for visibility and credibility, but it is less reliable as a direct pay-per-view engine. Monetization paths include gifts, subscriptions, branded content, affiliate-style selling, and product promotion, depending on eligibility and region.

Why it matters:

- Good place to build a recognizable personal/media brand.
- Reels can still get reach.
- Useful for sponsors because brands understand Instagram.

Challenge:

- Direct view payout is less straightforward than YouTube, Facebook, or TikTok.
- Audience growth can be uneven.

Best role in the strategy:

Use Instagram as a brand and trust layer. Repost short-form content, test visuals, and build a profile that looks credible to sponsors.

Source:

- Instagram Gifts announcement: https://about.fb.com/news/2023/02/earn-money-on-instagram-with-gifts/

### 6. X

Best use: Commentary, fast reactions, topic sourcing, and text-based authority.

X has shifted from its older Creator Revenue Sharing enrollment to Original Content Rewards. X says eligible creators earn based on qualified impressions from Premium users on the Home Timeline. Eligibility includes Premium subscription, account standing, original content, at least 500 verified followers, and at least 500,000 Home Timeline impressions from verified users in the last 90 days.

Why it matters:

- Strong for commentary and rapid topic testing.
- Original analysis can be rewarded.
- Text posts can become scripts for videos.

Challenge:

- Requires paying for Premium to qualify.
- Qualified impressions are not the same as all impressions.
- Program rules exclude copied, minimally modified, and misleading content.

Best role in the strategy:

Use X for short takes, source discovery, and testing angles. Since money is tight, do not rely on X payouts until Premium cost makes sense.

Source:

- X Original Content Rewards: https://help.x.com/en/using-x/original-content-rewards

## Practical Platform Strategy

The project should not chase every monetization program at once. The recommended structure is:

1. Create one original short-form video.
2. Post it to TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Facebook Reels, and Instagram Reels.
3. Convert the same idea into a short X post or thread when useful.
4. Track performance.
5. Turn winners into longer YouTube videos.

## What Pays For Reactions?

No major platform should be treated as paying directly for likes or reactions. Reactions matter because they can increase:

- Recommendation likelihood.
- Comment activity.
- Share rate.
- Watch time.
- Follower conversion.
- Qualified-view potential.
- Brand/sponsor interest.

For this project, reactions should be treated as signal, not income. The money comes later through qualified views, platform monetization, sponsorships, affiliate offers, subscriptions, products, or services.
