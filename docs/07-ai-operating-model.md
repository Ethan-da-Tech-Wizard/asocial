# 07 AI Operating Model

Last updated: September 14, 2026

## Operating Goal

The creator wants this to be as hands-off as reasonably possible. The system should therefore use AI as the production assistant, research assistant, script assistant, caption assistant, repurposing assistant, and analyst.

The creator should mainly handle:

- Approving topics.
- Adding lived experience.
- Recording voice or face when needed.
- Checking that claims are fair and accurate.
- Posting or scheduling.
- Reviewing weekly conclusions.

## Core Principle

AI should reduce effort, not erase the creator's voice. Fully generic AI content is a risk because major platforms reward original, authentic content and can penalize copied, repetitive, mass-produced, or misleading material.

The safest model is:

> AI drafts the system. The creator gives it a pulse.

## What AI Should Do

### Research

AI should:

- Find topic angles.
- Summarize official sources.
- Extract key claims.
- Identify why the topic matters.
- Suggest viewer impact.
- Prepare plain-English explanations.

AI should not:

- Invent facts.
- Pretend uncertainty is certainty.
- Use unverified screenshots or claims.
- Turn rumors into news.

### Ideation

AI should generate:

- Daily topic lists.
- Hooks.
- Contrarian but fair angles.
- Comment-response ideas.
- Follow-up ideas from top-performing posts.
- Long-form video expansions from short-form winners.

### Scripting

AI should draft:

- 30 to 90 second scripts.
- 60 to 120 second TikTok Creator Rewards-style scripts.
- 5 to 10 minute YouTube outlines.
- Caption variants.
- Title variants.
- Thumbnail text ideas.
- Calls to action.

The creator should review scripts for:

- Does this sound like me?
- Is this true enough to say publicly?
- Does it overpromise?
- Would I be comfortable defending this?

### Repurposing

One approved topic should become:

- TikTok video.
- YouTube Short.
- Facebook Reel.
- Instagram Reel.
- Optional X post or thread.
- Optional long-form YouTube outline if performance is strong.

AI should rewrite captions for each platform instead of copying the exact same caption everywhere.

### Analysis

Every week, AI should review:

- Best-performing posts.
- Worst-performing posts.
- Hooks that worked.
- Topics that created comments.
- Posts with high shares.
- Posts with high saves.
- Posts that gained followers.
- What to make next.

## Required Human Checkpoints

The system should keep human checkpoints at places where risk or trust matters.

Required checkpoints:

1. Before posting factual claims.
2. Before posting about legal, medical, financial, or high-stakes topics.
3. Before using another person's content.
4. Before making an accusation.
5. Before using realistic AI-generated media.
6. Before accepting sponsorships or affiliate offers.

## AI Disclosure And Platform Safety

YouTube requires disclosure when AI is used to meaningfully alter or generate realistic content that viewers could mistake for real people, places, scenes, or events. YouTube says creators generally do not need to disclose AI used for production assistance such as outlines, scripts, thumbnails, captions, or minor edits.

This project should avoid realistic fake scenes, fake people, fake events, and deepfake-style content. It should use AI mostly for writing, planning, editing, captions, and simple visuals.

## Hands-Off Workflow

### Daily Workflow

1. AI proposes 5 topics.
2. AI ranks them by urgency, audience fit, and ease.
3. Creator approves 1 topic.
4. AI drafts 3 hooks and 1 script.
5. Creator approves or lightly revises.
6. Creator records voice or face.
7. AI drafts caption and platform variants.
8. Creator posts or schedules.
9. Metrics are logged the next day.

### Batch Workflow

This is better once the creator has momentum.

1. AI generates 30 topics.
2. AI drafts 15 scripts.
3. Creator approves 7 to 10 scripts.
4. Creator records several videos in one session.
5. AI creates captions and posting variants.
6. Creator posts one per day.
7. AI does weekly review.

## Minimum Viable Human Effort

The realistic minimum is about 30 to 60 minutes per day during the first phase:

- 10 minutes approving topic and script.
- 10 to 20 minutes recording.
- 10 to 20 minutes posting and logging.
- 10 minutes reviewing comments or notes.

After templates improve, the daily effort may drop because scripts, captions, and formats become repeatable.

## Automation Limit

This cannot be completely hands-off at the beginning because the asset being built is trust. If the account sounds generic, wrong, copied, or faceless in the wrong way, it may get views but fail to build a monetizable audience.

The correct target is not zero work. The correct target is high-leverage work:

> The creator should spend effort only where human judgment, voice, trust, and lived experience matter.
